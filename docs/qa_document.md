# 🎓 Project Defense Q&A Document

**Project:** Hybrid Movie Recommendation System  
**Course:** Recommender Systems Practical Study, BSc Data Science & Analytics, JKUAT  
**Purpose:** Internal oral defense and technical presentation preparation. 15 questions, one owner per seat.

---

| # | Question | 2–3 Sentence Technical Answer | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Why did the team select MovieLens 100k (`ml-latest-small`), and what are its key statistical characteristics?** | MovieLens 100k provides a benchmark dataset with 100,836 ratings across 610 users and 9,742 movies, offering a matrix sparsity of 98.30%. This realistic sparsity mirrors commercial platforms where users interact with less than 2% of the catalog, while remaining computationally tractable for local training, cross-validation, and deep learning sweeps without dedicated GPU clusters. | Seat 1 |
| **2** | **Why was Leave-One-Out (LOO) chosen over k-fold cross-validation for ranking evaluation?** | Leave-One-Out holds out each user's single most recent interaction chronologically, preventing temporal data leakage where future interactions predict past behavior. Furthermore, LOO accurately replicates commercial deployment where the recommender must predict the user's immediate next consumption choice given their full historical sequence. | Seat 2 |
| **3** | **Why is an explicit rating threshold ($\ge 4.0$) applied to determine 'relevance' in ranking evaluation?** | In MovieLens, users frequently assign low ratings (0.5 to 2.5) to movies they disliked; recommending a movie that the user rated 1.0 would constitute a catastrophic user experience. By defining relevance strictly as true rating $\ge 4.0$, Recall@10 and NDCG@10 measure whether the model recommends films the user genuinely appreciated rather than merely films they watched. | Seat 2 |
| **4** | **Why does the Popularity baseline achieve a respectable Recall@10 (0.0634) despite lacking personalization?** | Movie consumption adheres to a Pareto power-law distribution where the top 100 blockbusters account for 16.1% of all ratings. Because many users' final test interaction happens to be a popular blockbuster, the popularity baseline captures these by brute force, though it fails completely on niche items and provides zero personalization. | Seat 3 |
| **5** | **How does User-Based Collaborative Filtering calculate neighborhood similarities, and how are rating biases handled?** | User-CF computes cosine similarity over mean-centered rating vectors ($R - \bar{r}_u$), ensuring that harsh critics who rate between 1–3 and lenient raters who rate between 3–5 are normalized to a common zero-mean scale. A shrinkage parameter ($\beta=5.0$) dampens similarity weights backed by few co-rated items to avoid spurious correlation between users with tiny overlaps. | Seat 4 |
| **6** | **What is Adjusted Cosine Similarity in Item-Based CF, and why is it preferred over raw item cosine similarity?** | Adjusted cosine similarity subtracts the corresponding user's average rating from each co-rated item pair, effectively eliminating user rating scale discrepancies across items. It calculates similarity along the user dimension rather than item dimension, yielding stable item neighborhoods that can be precomputed and cached for sub-millisecond retrieval. | Seat 5 |
| **7** | **How does SVD Matrix Factorization discover latent preferences, and why does it achieve the lowest RMSE (0.8831)?** | SVD decomposes the sparse user-item interaction matrix into lower-dimensional user ($P \in \mathbb{R}^{U \times k}$) and item ($Q \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times k}$) latent factor matrices along with global, user, and item bias terms ($\mu + b_u + b_i$). Because SVD optimizes directly for squared rating reconstruction error via regularized SGD, it minimizes prediction error on observed ratings better than heuristic neighborhood methods. | Seat 5 |
| **8** | **How does the Content-Based engine combine genres and user-applied tags into TF-IDF representations?** | Movie text representations concatenate pipe-delimited genres (prefixed with `g_` to prevent vocabulary collisions) with all user-applied tags in lowercase, and transforms them into an $L_2$-normalized TF-IDF vector with sublinear term frequency scaling. User preference profiles are formed as rating-weighted sums of movie vectors, enabling cosine scoring against all candidate movies. | Seat 6 |
| **9** | **How were the blend weights in the Weighted Hybrid tuned without leaking test set information?** | Model weights were tuned via a grid search on an independent validation split comprising each user's last 5 training ratings ($N_{\text{val}}=5$), maximizing validation Recall@10. The held-out test set was sequestered and evaluated strictly once at the end with weights locked ($w_{\text{user}}=0.60, w_{\text{item}}=0.40$), ensuring zero data leakage. | Seat 7 |
| **10** | **What is the NeuMF architecture in our Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) model, and how do GMF and MLP interact?** | NeuMF combines a Generalized Matrix Factorization (GMF) branch that captures linear latent vector element-wise products with a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) branch that learns non-linear feature interactions through dense layers and ReLU activations. The concatenated latent representations ($[g; m]$) feed into a final sigmoid projection layer trained with Binary Cross-Entropy loss. | Seat 8 |
| **11** | **Why did tuning NCF to smaller embedding dimensions (dim=16, layers 32-16-8) outperform larger dimensions (dim=64)?** | On MovieLens 100k, high-capacity architectures with 64-dimensional embeddings and 128-unit MLP layers suffer severe overfitting due to the modest sample size (100k interactions across 610 users). Smaller latent dimensions (dim=16) with 10% dropout act as strong regularizers, yielding higher validation recall (0.0824 vs 0.0754) and superior generalization. | Seat 9 |
| **12** | **What does NDCG@10 measure that Recall@10 ignores, and why did the Weighted Hybrid win on both?** | While Recall@10 treats all hits within the top 10 equally, Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG@10) penalizes relevant items that appear low in the list using a logarithmic rank discount ($\frac{1}{\log_2(r+1)}$). The Weighted Hybrid achieved the highest NDCG@10 (0.0521) because its blended scores consistently propelled relevant films to ranks 1–3 rather than ranks 8–10. | Seat 10 |
| **13** | **What did our empirical catalog coverage analysis reveal about popularity bias in recommender systems?** | Popularity baseline recommends only 122 unique movies across the entire user base (1.25% catalog coverage), whereas Content-Based covers 2,112 movies (21.68%) and Item-CF covers 1,015 movies (10.42%). The Weighted Hybrid achieves 401 unique recommendations (4.12% coverage), balancing high ranking precision with three times the catalog exploration of pure popularity. | Seat 11 |
| **14** | **How does the system address the Cold-Start Problem for new users with zero interaction history?** | When an unindexed user enters the system, collaborative filtering models cannot compute user neighborhoods or latent embeddings; the app seamlessly triggers content-based fallback. By eliciting either preferred genres or 1–3 previously enjoyed movies, the system projects the new user into TF-IDF vector space and returns instant, genre-aligned recommendations. | Seat 12 |
| **15** | **How does the Streamlit feedback loop (👍/👎) support ongoing model adaptation without violating user privacy?** | When users click 👍 or 👎 on recommendation cards, interaction logs append to `feedback.csv` containing anonymized user IDs, movie IDs, model tags, and timestamps without collecting PII. In future training cycles, positive feedback serves as additional implicit positive interactions, while negative feedback penalizes future candidate scoring. | Seat 13 / 14 |
